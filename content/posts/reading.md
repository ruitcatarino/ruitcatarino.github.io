# My Reading Story

Since I was a child my parents always endorsed and read to me but once I got the "power" of deciding what I wanted to do in my free time I stopped reading since it was "boring". I developed another hobbies like playing video games, listening to music and playing sports, in specific volleyball, reading just stopping being present in my life.

This went on until I was around 13 years old and a friend of mine recommended me to read *CHERUB*. To anyone that doesn't know *CHERUB* it's a 12 book series discussing teenagers or young adults being spies. I know I know, it seems a bit (or a lot) childish but at the time I was one and it was an awesome series to read. I remember being so addicted to the storyline that I spent an entire Saturday reading, just leaving my room to eat and even holding my bladder so I didn't waste any time (please don't do this).

I continued to read and eventually fished the entire 12 books series, proceeded to ask the same friend for another recommendation and he recommenced me *Conspiracy 365* and once again I was glued to it.
After that series I gave a chance to another book that I really didn't enjoy nor did it intrigued me and instead of just switching to a different book I just simply stopped reading.

Time passed and we are now in 2024, I was really into physics and space and wanted to dive deep into the matter, read from the best of the best and enter their mind, thoughts, and understandings. I decided to pick up *A Brief History of Time* by Stephen Hawking.

I'm going to be honest here, I'm a super slow reader, and I found out that reading nonfiction, remember that this was a first, was harder than I thought. It took me an entire month to finish it and at the end I didn't even enjoyed it that much. I achieved what I wanted, to understood more of the subject I wanted to learn from but it felt exactly like that, studying. It didn't remind me of how I felt when I was 13 that intense feeling of wanted to do nothing else except reading. And that's when I started to read fiction again and it turned out to be a path of no return.

I want to leave some of my 5 starts or top recommendation so far, so if you ever want to start reading (and share the same taste as I) I would advise one of the following:
- *Red Rising* trilogy (in specific the *Morning Star*) by Pierce Brown
- *Project Hail Mary* by Andy Weir
- *The Sword of Kaigen* by M.L. Wang
- *The Will of the Many* by James Islington
