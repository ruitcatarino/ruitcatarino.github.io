# My Reading Story

Since I was a child my parents always endorsed and read to me but once I got the "power" of deciding what I wanted to do in my free time I stopped reading since it was "boring". I developed another hobbies like playing video games, listening to music and playing sports, in specific volleyball, reading just stopping being present in my life.

This went on until I was around 13 years old and a friend of mine recommended me to read *CHERUB*. To anyone that doesn't know *CHERUB* it's a 12 book series discussing teenagers or young adults being spies. I know I know, it seems a bit (or a lot) childish but at the time I was one and it was an awesome series to read. I remember being so addicted to the storyline that I spent an entire Saturday reading, just leaving my room to eat and even holding my bladder so I didn't waste any time (please don't do this).

I continued to read and eventually fished the entire 12 books series, proceeded to ask the same friend for another recommendation and he recommenced me *Conspiracy 365* and once again I was glued to it.
After that series I gave a chance to another book that I really didn't enjoy nor did it intrigued me and instead of just switching to a different book I just simply stopped reading.

Time passed and we are now in 2024, I was really into physics and space and wanted to dive deep into the matter, read from the best of the best and enter their mind, thoughts, and understandings. I decided to pick up *A Brief History of Time* by Stephen Hawking.

I'm going to be honest here, I'm a super slow reader, and I found out that reading nonfiction, remember that this was a first, was harder than I thought. It took me an entire month to finish it and at the end I didn't even enjoyed it that much. I achieved what I wanted, to understood more of the subject I wanted to learn from but it felt exactly like that, studying. It didn't remind me of how I felt when I was 13 that intense feeling of wanted to do nothing else except reading. And that's when I started to read fiction again and it turned out to be a path of no return.

I want to leave some of my 5 stars or top recommendation so far, so if you ever want to start reading (and share the same taste as I) I would advise one of the following:
- *Red Rising* trilogy (in specific the *Morning Star*) by Pierce Brown
- *Project Hail Mary* by Andy Weir
- *The Sword of Kaigen* by M.L. Wang
- *The Will of the Many* by James Islington

# Why I Think Everyone Should Read

I think that everybody can recognize that reading in beneficial for themselves, but, as I once thought, reading can be perceived as an activity that is boring to do. If it seems and sometimes feels boring than why have I changed my mind and actually am now endorsing people around me and on the internet (well someone is reading this right?) to do it?

## Because It Can Feel Boring and Hard

One of the reasons is exactly that, because it can feel boring. I think that we live in times where everything is easily accessible and fast paced. If we are bored at the metro we pick up our phone to distract our mind. If we go out to eat everybody around us is staring at their screens and sometimes not even communicating with the person in front of them. If we have any question search it in the internet and get an immediate answer. With AI this is becoming even worse, have any doubt in life ask an AI agent. My point is we stopped doing hard things because every single one of us walks around with a dopamine machine on our pockets. Things that used to feel simple like reading this blog post now require an extreme focus. Reading forces you to slow down and appreciate hard things. At the end of your reading session you will feel much more accomplish than just scrolling a feed designed to captivate your attention span and consume you. Since I started reading anything that required the minimum of my attention returned to being simple and easy.

## We Are What We Consume

My view in life is that you as a person are what you were taught, lived and experienced. You don't know what you don't know. What I mean by this is if you were never taught that the Earth was round, you would look around and think it was flat. We only know it is because our ancestors worked hard, proved it and passed their knowledge and findings forward to us. The ability to write and read, in my view, played a huge part in this. TODO

I'm not gonna hide it or lie but while reading *The Sword of Kaigen* by M.L. Wang I cried. I'm not gonna share spoilers but there is an intense emotional part on the book that brought tears down my cheeks. I never lived that moment but just by reading it I can resent with it. Same things happened in other books, I have felt excited, angry, sad, hopeful, tense, and many other emotions while reading. My point is that reading made me more empathic. I didn't need to live or experience it myself, I was being fully transported into the story and world described in said book and feeling like I lived it.
